      Learn the basics

      Short answers to common questions about taxes on NIL and revenue-share money, each with a link to the IRS page it comes from.

      Is NIL and revenue-share money taxable?

      Generally, yes. The IRS says all income from NIL activities is generally taxable, and it lists revenue-sharing payments from a college or university as NIL income. You have to report it even if the payer doesn't send you a Form 1099.

      IRS: What is NIL income?
      Why wasn't any tax taken out?

      If you're paid as self-employed, the IRS says there's typically no tax withheld from NIL income. Setting the tax part aside is then up to you.

      If your school treats you as an employee, tax is taken out of your pay and you get a Form W-2. Not sure which applies to you? Check your pay stub or deposit details, or ask your athletic department.

      IRS: What is NIL income?
      What is self-employment tax?

      It's Social Security and Medicare tax for people who work for themselves: 15.3% in total (12.4% Social Security and 2.9% Medicare), figured on Schedule SE. The IRS says payments for personal services carry it, but royalties and image licenses don't.

      Half of it can be deducted when you figure your adjusted gross income. That's why SetAside subtracts half of it before working out income tax.

      IRS: Self-employment tax IRS: What is NIL income?
      How do tax brackets work?

      Income tax goes up in steps. Each rate applies only to the part of your income inside that bracket, not to all of it.

      For a single filer in 2025, the first $11,925 of taxable income is taxed at 10%, the part up to $48,475 at 12%, up to $103,350 at 22%, then 24%, 32%, 35% and 37% above $626,350. Taxable income is what's left after the standard deduction, which is $15,750 for a single filer in 2025.

      IRS: Tax rates and brackets IRS: Standard deduction
      What are estimated tax payments?

      When no tax is withheld, the IRS says you may need to pay estimated tax during the year with Form 1040-ES. Doing so may help you avoid a large tax bill and related penalties.

      SetAside doesn't track payment deadlines or pay the IRS for you. It shows how much to keep aside so the money is there when you pay.

      Why does SetAside ask for my income for the whole year?

      Because your tax rate depends on your total income for the year, not on one payment. SetAside estimates your tax for the year, turns it into a share of your income, and sets aside that share of each payment.

      So a better yearly guess gives a better estimate. My payments shows your income so far, and the next payment you enter starts with your last guess.

      SetAside uses 2025 federal figures. This is general information, not tax advice. Check with a tax professional about your own situation.
